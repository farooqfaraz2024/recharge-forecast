Method and caveats

  • Structure. Recharging customers per day = active base × share who recharge + tourist customers. Revenue = recharging customers × value per customer.
  • Share who recharge is learned from history: a level for the forecast month, plus day-of-week, salary-cycle and event effects, a difference for other calendar months, and a year-on-year drift.
  • Bayesian. Each effect starts from a prior (how big it can plausibly be) and is updated by the data. The result is thousands of plausible parameter sets, not one best guess.
  • Simulation. Each parameter set is combined with one draw of every business input. The active base is rolled forward day by day with gross adds and churn. Monthly totals are added up inside each simulation before ranges are taken.
  • Robust to odd days. The model uses heavy-tailed (Student-t) noise, so an outage or data glitch does not distort it.
  • Limits. With only two months of history, the year-on-year drift and the season difference are not separately known; their priors set how much doubt is carried into the forecast. Adding more months narrows the range. Events not listed in the settings (a new tariff, a campaign, a network incident) are not in the forecast.
